THE FIX, AND WHERE IT CAME FROM
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Module 6 error analysis showed that plain RRF lost all 8 of the failure
cases where e5-base alone had found the gold passage. The cause is structural,
not a tuning problem.

BM25 can only return passages sharing a script with the query: a Romanized or
Devanagari query and a passage in the other script share essentially no tokens.
In plain RRF a passage found by both systems collects two contributions and one
found only by the dense retriever collects one. Cross-script passages are
therefore docked roughly 2:1 for the LEXICAL RETRIEVER'S BLINDNESS rather than
for their own irrelevance. BM25 saying nothing about a Devanagari passage, given
a Latin query, is not evidence against it -- it is no evidence at all.

script_aware_rrf scores each passage over the systems that were ELIGIBLE to
retrieve it, so a cross-script candidate is judged on the dense contribution
alone instead of being penalised for a vote that was never possible.

H2 REVISITED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The alpha sweep said H2 was not supported: no interior alpha beat both endpoints
by more than 0.001. That verdict was correct about the method it tested and
wrong about the hypothesis. Hybrid retrieval DOES beat its own components --
script-aware fusion beats dense alone overall at p=0.0086 -- but only once fusion
stops penalising passages for being in the language the query was not written in.

The honest cost is the monolingual regression: -0.044 at p=0.032. Removing the
lexical double-vote costs a little precision where both retrievers could see the
same passage. On this corpus that is a good trade, because monolingual retrieval
was already strong and cross-lingual was near zero, but it is a trade and not a
free win.

This came out of reading twenty error cases, not out of a metric. The aggregate
tables showed hybrid underperforming and gave no hint as to why.

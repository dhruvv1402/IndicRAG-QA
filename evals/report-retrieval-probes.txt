FINDINGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lexical retrieval collapses across the language boundary. BM25 Recall@5 is
   0.006 cross-lingual and 0.028 code-mixed, against 0.740 monolingual. A
   Romanized or Devanagari query and an English passage share almost no tokens,
   so there is nothing for BM25 to match. This is H4, and it is not marginal.
2. Dense retrieval does cross that boundary, but is weak in absolute terms here:
   e5-base reaches 0.125 cross-lingual and 0.132 code-mixed. Twenty times BM25
   on the cross-lingual slice, and still far below the 0.70 target in PRD S10.2.
3. Overall BM25 still leads (0.499 vs e5-base 0.449, p=0.10, not significant).
   That is an artefact of the probe mix: 120 of 180 probes are monolingual and
   the fragment shape quotes the passage directly. It is not evidence against H1.
4. Hybrid weighted fusion is the only arm that improves on BM25 overall
   (+0.025, p=0.13) and it does so without losing the monolingual slice. The
   improvement is inside the noise on this probe set.

The honest summary: these probes establish that the pipeline works and that the
cross-lingual gap is real and large. They cannot rank the encoders fairly, and
no claim about H1 or H2 should rest on them. That needs the gold set (P3).

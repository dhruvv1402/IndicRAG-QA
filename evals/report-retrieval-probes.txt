FINDINGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. H4 SUPPORTED, strongly. Lexical retrieval does not cross the language
   boundary at all. BM25 Recall@5 is 0.740 monolingual, 0.006 cross-lingual,
   0.028 code-mixed. A Devanagari or Romanized query and an English passage
   share almost no tokens, so there is nothing for BM25 to match.

2. H6 SUPPORTED. MuRIL, the Indic-pretrained model the brief names, is the
   WORST dense encoder tested: 0.238 mean-pooled, 0.143 CLS-pooled, against
   0.303 for MiniLM -- a model with half the parameters but trained for
   retrieval. Both poolings were run and the better is reported, so this is not
   a pooling artefact. Most striking: MuRIL/mean scores exactly 0.000 on both
   the cross-lingual and the code-mixed slices. The model pretrained on 17
   Indian languages is the one that cannot cross between two of them.
   Pretraining-language coverage does not substitute for retrieval training.

3. Unexpected, and contrary to the prediction in ARCHITECTURE S7.1. LaBSE was
   expected to lead the cross-lingual slice and does not (0.097, below MiniLM
   at 0.136 and e5-base at 0.125). Instead it dominates CODE-MIXED by a wide
   margin: 0.332, against 0.132 for e5-base and 0.028 for BM25 -- 2.5x the next
   best system and 12x the lexical baseline. LaBSE is the only model that
   handles Romanized Hindi well. This wants confirming on the gold set before
   it goes in the paper, but if it holds it is the most useful practical
   finding here: pick the encoder by query SCRIPT, not by language coverage.

4. H1 NOT ESTABLISHED on these probes. BM25 leads overall (0.499 vs e5-base
   0.449, p=0.10, not significant). That is an artefact of the probe mix --
   120 of 180 are monolingual and the fragment shape quotes the passage. It is
   not evidence against H1, and it is not evidence for it either.

5. H2 NOT SUPPORTED. The best interior alpha beats the best endpoint by 0.001,
   one query changing its mind. Hybrid weighted fusion is nonetheless the only
   arm that improves on BM25 overall (+0.025, p=0.13) while holding the
   monolingual slice, so it is worth re-testing on the gold set.

The honest summary: the pipeline works, the cross-lingual gap is real and large,
and two negative results (MuRIL, hybrid) plus one surprise (LaBSE on code-mixed)
are already visible. These probes cannot rank encoders fairly and no claim in
the paper should rest on them. That needs the gold set (P3).
